Threats to Biodiversity
and the Healthy Society

A Research Paper in Science, Technology, and Society · BSIT 2-03 Group 2

Introduction. Biodiversity refers to the variety of all living organisms on Earth, including plants, animals, and microorganisms, and the ecosystems they form. It plays a vital role in Science, Technology, and Society by providing essential services such as food, clean water, medicine, climate regulation, and disease control. Advances in science and technology have helped us understand and conserve biodiversity, while society depends on it for health, livelihood, and overall well-being.

However, global biodiversity is under severe threat. Major issues include habitat loss, climate change, pollution, overexploitation of resources, and invasive species. These threats not only endanger ecosystems but also affect human health and societal stability. Given the direct connection between a healthy environment and a healthy society, it is important to study the global threats to biodiversity and how they impact human life. This research paper aims to explore these threats and their implications to Science, Technology, and Society.

Main Headline

"The Silent Crisis: How Biodiversity Loss Threatens Global Health"

By BSIT 2-03 Group 2 · 2026

According to the IPBES Global Assessment Report (2019), nature is declining globally at rates unprecedented in human history. Land and sea use changes, direct exploitation, climate change, pollution, and invasive alien species are the major direct drivers of biodiversity loss. This headline article synthesizes our group's findings: the rapid decline of species isn't just an environmental issue — it directly fuels the spread of infectious diseases, reduces food security, and undermines mental well-being. The message is clear: protecting nature is protecting ourselves.

The Five Major Threats

1. Habitat Destruction

Research finding

Deforestation and land conversion for agriculture and settlements destroy ecosystems, forcing wildlife into closer contact with humans and increasing zoonotic disease risks.

2. Pollution

Health implication

Plastic waste, chemicals, and industrial pollutants contaminate ecosystems. Marine animals get entangled in plastic, while contaminated water exposes humans to diseases.

3. Climate Change

Current issue

Changing temperatures and extreme weather disrupt ecosystems. Coral bleaching and habitat fragmentation threaten food security and coastal safety.

4. Overexploitation

Environmental implication

Illegal wildlife trade, overfishing, and excessive logging deplete species faster than they can recover, threatening food supplies and marine balance.

5. Invasive Species

Expert perspective

Non-native species introduced through trade outcompete native wildlife, disrupt food webs, and introduce new diseases to ecosystems.

Chapter 3: Research Design & Data Gathering

This study utilized a descriptive research design to understand the threats to biodiversity and their effects on society. 1. Deforestation and Habitat Destruction

Deforestation and habitat destruction are major threats to biodiversity. Forests, wetlands, mangroves, and other natural habitats provide food, shelter, and breeding areas for numerous species. When these habitats are cleared for agriculture, settlements, roads, mining, and other human activities, plants and animals may lose the resources they need to survive.

Case Study: Kalimantan & Sumatra

In Indonesia, peatland forest is cleared for paper and pulpwood. Devastating fires in 2015 peaked in 2016, with smoke hiding the loss in satellite images. Furthermore, in the Eastern Himalayas, over 75% of the original habitat has been destroyed, pushing species like snow leopards and red pandas toward extinction.

Related Study (Newbold et al., 2015)

Their study, published in Nature, found that human activities such as intensive land use and related environmental pressures are associated with reductions in local biodiversity.

2. Pollution and Improper Waste Disposal

Pollution is another major threat to biodiversity. Plastic waste, chemicals, sewage, pesticides, and industrial pollutants can contaminate land and water ecosystems. Marine animals may become entangled in plastic waste or mistake it for food.

Restoration Efforts

Organizations like NOAA allocated $28 million in 2022 to restore marine resources damaged by pollution, including oil spills, ship groundings, and hazardous waste sites.

Conservation Measure

Reducing pollution through proper waste disposal, wastewater treatment, and responsible use of chemicals can help protect biodiversity and human health.

3. Climate Change

Climate change affects biodiversity by changing temperature patterns, rainfall, ocean conditions, and the frequency or intensity of certain extreme weather events. Coral bleaching, for example, can occur when unusually warm ocean temperatures stress corals.

4. Overexploitation of Natural Resources

Overexploitation occurs when plants, animals, fish, timber, or other natural resources are collected or used faster than they can recover. Examples include illegal wildlife trade, overfishing, excessive logging, and unsustainable hunting.

5. Invasive Alien Species

Invasive alien species are organisms introduced outside their natural range that spread and cause harm to native biodiversity, ecosystems, the economy, or human health.

Synthesis: The Global Crisis

According to the IPBES Global Assessment Report (2019), nature is declining globally at rates unprecedented in human history. The Living Planet Report 2024 documented a 73% average decline in monitored wildlife populations between 1970 and 2020.
